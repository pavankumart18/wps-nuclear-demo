01 · Situation

Nuclear Welding is Unforgiving — and the Process Hasn't Changed in Decades

NPCIL SM-11 operations run three shifts per day. Every shift, supervisors must match qualified welders to critical pressure-boundary joints — cross-checking WPS documents, qualification tickets, continuity records, and shift rosters by hand.

📋
Regulatory Framework
ASME Section IX and IS 2825 require every welder to hold a valid, in-scope qualification ticket before touching a production weld. Process, material group, thickness, position, and PWHT must all align.
📁
Document Sprawl
WPS documents live as PDF files. Qualification records live in a separate register. Continuity logs are maintained in spreadsheets. No system connects them at shift time.
👷
Human Dependency
Assignment correctness depends entirely on the Welding Engineer's memory and attention. When the senior engineer is absent, junior staff may not know every edge case — thickness limits, position coverage, expiry windows.
02 · Complication

Three Failure Modes that Cost Real Money — and Carry Real Risk

The current process is not merely slow. It creates three categories of failure with direct financial, safety, and regulatory consequences.

The core problem: a qualified welder is not the same as a qualified welder for this job. A welder might hold a valid SMAW ticket but on the wrong P-number, or a full-range ticket that has lapsed continuity, or a limited-range ticket that doesn't cover the joint thickness. Manual checking catches these only if the checker knows exactly what to look for — and has time.
⚠️
Non-Conformance Risk
Assigning an out-of-scope welder creates a non-conformance record that triggers a full weld repair, post-weld inspection, and root-cause documentation. On a pressure boundary, that means system downtime.
⏱️
Shift Delays
Average manual cross-check: 25–40 minutes per complex job. For an 80-job shift with 12 WPS documents in scope, that time compounds. Critical path jobs wait while paperwork is verified.
🔍
Audit Gaps
Current process leaves no structured audit trail. Who checked which constraint, which data version, at what time — is unrecorded. External auditors have no digital evidence chain to review.

03 · Resolution

A Seven-Step AI Workflow that Encodes the Welding Engineer's Knowledge

WeldAssign AI doesn't replace the engineer — it gives them a tool that never forgets a constraint, never skips a check, and always produces a printable audit artifact.

Step 1
Shift Planner
Select job from 80-job queue, filtered by shift / priority / WPS
Step 2
WPS Extraction
AI reads WPS PDF — extracts 12 qualification fields at 97% confidence
Step 3
Job Validation
8-constraint check: material, thickness, position, PWHT, PQR, progression
Step 4
Qual Mapping
Preferred + alternate qualification ticket identified from matrix
Step 5
Welder Matching
All 50 welders scored — ranked by compatibility with rejection explanations
Step 6
Supervisor Approval
1-click approval with full constraint evidence available on demand
Step 7
Execution Ticket
Printable ticket: job + WPS + welder + pass parameters + signatures
Key design principle: every decision is traceable to a specific data field, qualification record, or rule. The system never makes an invisible assumption. A welder rejected for thickness mismatch sees exactly which ticket covers which range — and what ticket they would need to be eligible.
